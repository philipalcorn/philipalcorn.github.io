I'm an electrical engineering student at [Tarleton State University](https://www.tarleton.edu/) (B.S. expected May 2027, 4.0 GPA). 
My research interests broadly lie in the field of **computer architecture**.
Generally speaking, I am interested in a hardware/software co-design approach
to improving the performance of computational workloads, such as AI/ML
and scientific computing. Most of my research experience has been in
scientific HPC, but I am interested in a wide range of topics and have 
worked in several different labs at Tarleton. 

Research interests
------
These are some of the broad questions I would like to investigate in my 
future graduate research:
- Workload-driven architecture: using information known about runtime execution (what do the memory access patterns like like? What operations are causing bottlenecks?) to guide improvements in CPUs/GPUs/Accelerators:
- Domain-specific accelerators, particularly for sparse and irregular scientific workloads
- Exploring trade-offs between software and hardware optimization (at what point should hardware acceleration be considered and what tradeoffs are made in that decision?)
- Modeling and simulation techniques for evaluating proposed hardware designs

Recent Work / Projects
------
- **GPU molecular dynamics (NSF REU, Michigan State, 2026):** Built *TriChimes*, a Triton GPU implementation of the ChIMES machine-learned interatomic potential, capable of simluating 1 million atoms in just over 3 seconds per timestep. Advised by Dr. Dylan Anstine.  
- **Cardiac digital twin (Tarleton HPC Lab, 2025–2026):** Extended a  near-real-time model of the human left atrium to automatically search for fibrosis patterns likely to cause arrhythmias using Monte Carlo techniques. Advised by Dr. Bryant Wyatt.
- **FPGA CNN accelerator (senior capstone project, in progress):** Designing a convolution accelerator that classifies live video on a Terasic DE1-SoC.
